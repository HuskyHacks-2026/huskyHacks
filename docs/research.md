## Sound Credit Union (shared institution)

### What is public

- Tacoma cooperative, founded 1940, charter 66331
- About **$3.07B** audited assets at December 31, 2024
- Call report for June 30, 2026: **$3.40B** assets, **184,758** members, **26** offices, well capitalized
- Sound’s September 14, 2026 release: over **185,000** members
- Jack Henry Symitar core
- Candescent digital banking, expanded November 19, 2024 for account opening, credit scores, and continuous verification
- Falcon monitors cards
- Business account opening went live June 2025
- Washington Business Bank acquired December 3, 2024 (~800 business clients)

### What the controls actually stop

Sound’s security page says continuous verification uses behavior, location, and device signals against people who already have the username and password. Falcon calls about unusual card spend and will not ask for a PIN.

That is **unauthorized use**. Imposter scams are the **member**, on a known device, sending their **own** money. MFA succeeds. The gap is the **instruction**, not the credential.

### Citations to avoid

- Do not cite the $119 million Sound Federal Credit Union.
- *Fealy v. Sound* was a collateral-protection insurance case, not a cyber incident.
- No public source states Sound’s internal fraud-loss total. Saying “Sound lost $X” would be an invention.

### Loss patterns relevant to a credit-union demo

| Finding | Who it hits | Mechanism | Why existing tools miss it |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Bank impersonation, FTC 2025 | Consumers; highest business-impersonation losses | Fake security alert, then move money to “protect” it | The member authenticates and authorizes |
| Credit union ATO, GoWest Jul 9 2025 | Credit unions; six- and seven-figure losses | Spoofed texts/calls, stolen OTPs, call-center resets | MFA is the thing being socially engineered |
| Same-institution mules | The receiving credit union | New account, member-to-member transfers, fast cash-out | Card fraud systems watch the merchant, not the internal mule |
| Fresh business accounts | Business onboarding | Entity filed days earlier, stolen Treasury check | Consumer KYC does not see a shell company |
| NCUA incident year | Credit union system | 539 reported incidents to Apr 30, 2025; none systemic | Confirms volume without naming Sound |

GoWest’s source is Ken Otsuka of TruStage, writing for the association that covers Washington. Posh separately writes that a core-connected voice agent becomes an account-takeover target once it can move money — a reason **not** to build a weekend voice bot in front of Karan Kashyap.

### Field note: post-compromise recovery

**Source type:** informal practitioner note (team contact’s brother — accountant at an independent firm in Vancouver). Not a published study. Use for problem texture and recovery criteria, not as a cited industry statistic.

| Theme | What they described |
| --- | --- |
| Credential / rail compromise | Client routing / ACH information gets obtained and compromises the entire checking account. Canceling debit cards is not enough — money can still be pulled electronically. |
| Backend scramble | After fraud, the accountant helps (1) get a **new account** set up and (2) **swap** business finances onto it: bills, payroll, supplies, rent, utilities. |
| Tooling gap | To their knowledge there are few (if any) tools that help with this recovery / rewiring work. Prevention and member education matter; clients often miss the risk until it is too late. |
| Credit tracking | Separately, they must track all fraud charges/expenses so the bank correctly credits the client back. |

**Implication for our brief:** prevention at payment time (Hold Line) stops the worst case. The judging criterion “recovery or member support” is still real — new account, payee/payroll remapping, and reimbursement ledger are the day-after problem. Good as a pitch phase-two / stretch story; do not let it displace the weekend hold demo.
